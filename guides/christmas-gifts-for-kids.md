The toys kids keep coming back to are usually open ended: things they build, make or imagine with, not things that do one trick. Find the age group, then check the box for the recommended age before you buy.

## Ages 3 to 5

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**Magnetic building tiles.** They snap together easily for small hands, and the same set builds towers, houses and marble runs for years. Bigger sets mean less fighting between siblings.

</div>

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**A play kitchen accessory set or toy tool bench.** Pretend play is the big thing at this age. Pots, pans and play food stretch a kitchen they already have; a tool bench suits kids who copy everything a parent does.
{% include amz.html q="toddler toy tool bench" %}
</div>

## Ages 6 to 8

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**A big box of building bricks, not a single model.** Themed sets get built once and shelved. A mixed classic box gets rebuilt into something new every weekend.
{% include amz.html q="classic building bricks creative box" %}
</div>

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**A first family board game.** Pick one with short rounds and simple rules so the whole family plays together over the holidays.
{% include amz.html q="family board game ages 6 and up" %}
</div>

## Ages 9 to 12

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**A science or engineering kit.** Crystal growing, circuits or a build your own robot. Look for kits where the finished thing actually works and can be taken apart again.
{% include amz.html q="snap circuits kit" %}
</div>

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**A book series, not one book.** If they liked the first in a series, the box set disappears in a week and keeps them off screens in the quiet days after Christmas.
{% include amz.html q="kids book box set ages 9-12" %}
</div>

## For any age

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
**Art supplies in a proper case.** A box of decent pencils, markers and a sketchbook feels more grown up than loose crayons, and gets used far more than you would expect.
{% include amz.html q="kids art supplies case" %}
</div>

## Quick tips

- Ask parents what they already have. Duplicates are the most common Christmas morning letdown.
- Check if batteries are needed and buy them now.
- Experiences count too: a cinema trip or swimming lessons can sit in a card under the tree.
